# Draft: request for CIB Data Hub statistics as open data

**Status: draft for the maintainer to review. Not sent.**

- **To:** psi@ciboard.ie
- **Subject:** Re-use request: Data Hub CIS and CIPS statistics in CSV or XLSX

Dear Citizens Information Board,

I maintain Ireland MCP, an open-source, read-only service that helps people and AI
assistants find Irish public data (https://irishopendata.ie/,
https://github.com/c-mongan/ireland-mcp). It now republishes the national annual
figures from your Data Hub statistical reports for 2021–2025 under CC BY 4.0, with your
required attribution and a link to each source report.

At present these figures are published as PDF and DOCX reports. To transcribe them, we
parse the Word tables and check each figure against the PDF edition. Under the European
Union (Open Data and Re-use of Public Sector Information) Regulations 2021 (S.I. No.
376 of 2021), I would like to request the following, if available:

1. The Citizens Information Services (CIS) annual and quarterly caller and query tables
   from 2021 onward in CSV or XLSX: callers, queries, query categories and
   sub-categories, mode of contact, and caller profile tables.
2. The Citizens Information Phone Service (CIPS) annual and quarterly tables in the same
   format: contacts by mode, and calls by category and sub-category.
3. If these tables are already held as data, regional or CIS-company breakdowns at
   whatever level you consider appropriate to publish.
4. An annual 2025 CIS callers total, if one exists. We currently derive it as the sum of
   the four 2025 quarterly reports (384,091) and label it as derived.

We also noticed some points you may wish to clarify in the reports:

- The CIPS "calls answered" figure includes webchat and social-media contacts in
  2021–2023 but only telephone calls in 2024–2025.
- In the 2025 CIPS report, three sub-category table totals differ slightly from the main
  table: Housing 6,382 vs 6,381, Money and Tax 6,014 vs 6,012, and Moving Country 4,108
  vs 4,105.
- The 2023 CIPS contact-mode table is headed "(Q1 2023)", but it contains annual figures.

Machine-readable files would let other researchers and services use these figures
without re-typing them, and would reduce the risk of transcription errors. I am happy to
share our transcriptions and checksums, published at
https://irishopendata.ie/data/cib/, if that would help.
